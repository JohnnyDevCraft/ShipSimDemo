# Communications Control

The communications officer's console, rebuilt on the Helm design (2026-10-29). It replaces the three-column preview (`preview.js`, `map.js`, `conversations.js`); the rules are the same. The signal map fills the window and every control floats over it. The page scales as one piece from an iPad in landscape (the smallest size) to 4K; it is not laid out for portrait. `tests/communications-layout.cjs` checks those sizes and `tests/communications.cjs` the behaviour.

Files: `index.html`, `styles.css`, `gto-data.js` (the chart: stars, planets, moons and stations) and `comms.js`, minified with `../Shared/console-ui.js` from `demo-app/static-src/` by `node scripts/build-static.cjs`; `../Shared/console.css` is the shared console look. `view-screen-discovery.md` describes the view screen settings.

- **Signal map (the whole window):** the chart, every detected transmission (red points; a selected one pulses, and a point-to-point link is drawn between its ends) and our ship (green), on a grid fixed to the screen with range circles and a galactic compass rose, as on Navigation Control; 000 points to the galactic centre, up the map. Drag to pan, pinch or scroll to zoom, tap to select, double-tap to centre. The line above the bottom bar describes the selection: galactic and system bearing and distance, and its distance from us.
- **Detected transmissions (left):** ALL, DISTRESS (distress calls and all clears), NETWORK (what the array overhears) or OURS (hails, replies, clearances and deliveries to our ship), as on the live console. Each card gives the medium, type, open or encrypted, the origin, system, frequency, destination (point to point), origin coordinates and when it was heard. Tap one to select it (again to clear). Below: SUBSPACE LISTENING (ARRAY OFFLINE with the array's relay open), the RF detection range and our array's reach, with any critical damage.
- **Space Traffic (live 1.0.0-Beta10):** network messages heard on the subspace network, listed with their channel and kind (notice, departure, arrival, flight plan, patrol report, report, navigation warning, dispatch, distress call, all clear, stand down, requisition) and messages to our ship (hail reply, hail refused, docking clearance, delivery approved or refused). Our organization's (Terran Space Command's) encrypted traffic is DECRYPTED ("ENCRYPTED · OUR ORGANIZATION" on the right); another organization's arrives without its text. Three are heard before the console opens and the rest arrive about every half minute while the array listens. A **distress call** raises a banner over the map (caller, GBEAR and distance from us, the call) with PLOT COURSE (the demo says what Navigation would plot: the demo's Navigation flies its own chart, so nothing is sent), HAIL and DISMISS; it goes at the caller's all clear, and a fresh MAYDAY brings it back after DISMISS. A docking clearance shows "Docking clearance held" in that station's chat.
- **Transmission (right):** the selected transmission's medium, name, received signal trace, type, channel, origin, destination or system, and the received content (hidden when encrypted), with Contact Origin.
- **Map range (right):** 100 km to 30 LY on a vertical slider.
- **Bottom bar:**
  - **Frequencies:** the standard frequencies as an overlay: Subspace or RF, a search box typed on the console keyboard, and one card per frequency; ships on the map add their advertised return channels. Tap one to open a communication on it.
  - **Communications:** the active communications and, for the chosen one, its setup (recipient; unicast, multicast or point to point; subspace or RF; the channel on the keypad; encryption) or, once opened, the chat with a message composer. New and End. A badge counts the active communications.
    - **Relay route** (subspace only, 2026-11-02): the hops from our ship through communications buoys to the recipient, each with its distance and whether it carries, and the state — DIRECT · IN REACH, ROUTE OPEN, OUT OF REACH, or RECIPIENT NOT ON THE CHART. **Add relay** lists the buoys not yet on the route, nearest the route's last end first, each IN REACH or OUT OF REACH of it (with its distance to the recipient) and whose it is. A buoy of our organization or an ally's is added at once: the computer holds its access code and fills it in. Any other asks for its code on the console keyboard; there is no way to learn one yet, so it cannot be used (a wrong code is refused). **Remove last** and **Direct** take buoys off. Open Communication is refused while a recipient on the chart is out of reach, saying which hop fails. The route is drawn on the signal map (dashed, red from the first hop that does not carry), and the chat's details end "via … → …". RF takes no relays.
    - **Keyboard or Voice** (in the overlay's header) chooses how the recipient and messages are entered. Keyboard opens the console keyboard; Voice opens a microphone panel in its place and the browser's speech recognition writes what is said into the field, showing words as they are heard; Clear and Done. The choice is remembered on the device. Voice needs a browser with speech recognition (Chrome, Edge or Safari) and microphone permission; otherwise the panel says so and points to the keyboard. Chrome and Edge send the audio to their speech service, so they need a network connection.
  - **Contact:** our subspace return channel and whether we broadcast it. The top bar shows BROADCAST and the channel, or LISTENING.
  - **View Screen:** sets up the bridge view screen (`../ViewScreen/`): view type (SRS, LRS, DUAL, or NONE for the forward scope alone), SRS and LRS ranges, the forward scope's zoom (1× to 20×), and which labels and objects it shows. Saved with `../Shared/view-screen-settings.js` where the View Screen reads it, so an open View Screen changes at once.
  - **30 LY View** and **Our Ship** move the map.
- **Communications:** the setup lists the contacts the recipient matches, nearest first, to tap; the chat's ROUTE goes back to the setup to change the relay route, keeping the conversation. A hail is refused at the console with the array off, out of reach as routed, or under critical damage.
- **Contact settings:** also the communications array: its name and slot, TX and RX reach ("n of design" when halved), power, health, critical damage and the ARRAY RELAY switch (open: the array is off and hears nothing).
- **Critical damage** (menu → Critical damage, shared by every console): no subspace (top bar NO SUBSPACE, route NO SUBSPACE · CRITICAL DAMAGE, hails refused, no traffic heard), subspace range halved, no RF (RF detections not heard, RF communications refused).
- **Menu → Demo controls:** hear the next network message, a distress call or its all clear, detect a sample transmission, set the RF detection range, choose our communications array's reach (Mark I 3 LY, Mark II 6 LY, Mark III 10 LY) and see whose buoys are whose. Reset demo restores the seven sample transmissions and clears the communications.

## Relay rules (the simulator's subspace routes, John 2026-10-01)

- A subspace message crosses a hop only within the sender's transmit reach (Tx) **and** the receiver's receive reach (Rx); replies cross the same hops back, so a two-way link needs both directions on every hop.
- The ten Terran communications buoys of the simulator's catalog, each beside its system's star: 20 LY Tx and Rx, all belonging to Terran Space Command (John, 2026-11-02). Our ship serves Terran Space Command, so the computer knows every code; a rival's or an enemy's buoy would ask for one we do not have. Terran Space Command (on Earth) 15 LY; stations and planets' settlements 5 LY; ships of the sample traffic 6 LY; our array 3 LY to start.
- Our ship is at Ross 128, 11 LY from Earth: Terran Space Command is out of reach directly, and in reach through the Ross 128 and Sol buoys.

## Rules (from the preview)

- A detection is listed for five minutes after it is heard, while one of its ends is on the map and, for RF, while it is within the RF detection range (100,000,000 km by default). Subspace has no range limit.
- Unicast uses channel 0. Multicast and point to point need a channel other than 0. Only point to point can be encrypted.
- Contact Origin opens a point-to-point communication on the transmission's advertised return channel (or its frequency), with the received content as the first message. A communication already open on the same channel and medium is reused.
- Opening a communication starts the demo's scripted exchange with the recipient (Terran Space Command when none is given). Messages we send are added to the chat; on a subspace unicast they carry our return channel.
- Standard frequencies for a system are listed while that system is on the map; general distress and fleet operations always are.
- A transmission's ends are where its detection says: an origin or destination that is our ship or a named station, planet or star is drawn exactly there; a ship the chart does not hold is placed near its system's station, clear of the star. A destination with no known place ("Unresolved") is not drawn. (The preview placed origins beside the station and sent every point-to-point link to the next system over, whatever the destination.)

Changes from the preview: Send now adds the message to the chat (the preview's final script left it doing nothing), the dictation button, which did nothing, is replaced by working voice entry, and a frequency tapped in the overlay opens a multicast communication on its channel (the preview opened unicast, which forced the channel to 0).

## Places' frequencies (MissionProtocol "Settlement and Base Communications", John 2026-11-12)

Frequencies lists, before the standard channels, the frequencies places answer on — each for the place itself, an organization's base there
or an organization's seat of government — with its medium and whether it reaches us: subspace within our array's reach (or through relay
buoys), RF within a light-hour, with its light time each way. Tranquility (Unity), the Commonwealth's base and seat of government, answers on
subspace: Public information, Traffic control, Commonwealth Command and the Commonwealth's government (and Public information on RF 326 for
its own system). A hail on RF in reach is in transit for the light time and the reply follows after the same time back; RF farther than a
light-hour is refused, offering subspace. Who answers follows the frequency: the place's desk, the organization's command, or its government.
